## 1. Edition Identified

* **Exact Title:** *Herrn von Leibniz' Rechnung mit Null und Eins* (also cataloged as *Herrn von Leibniz' Rechnung mit 0 und 1*)
* **Editors / Authors:** Erich Hochstetter, Hermann-Josef Greve, Heinz Gumin
* **Translators:** Pater Franz X. Wernz S.J., Dr. Rudolf Soellner (Latin to German translation)
* **Publisher:** Siemens Aktiengesellschaft (Berlin & München)
* **First Edition Year:** 1966 (published on the occasion of the 250th anniversary of Leibniz's death; 2nd edition in 1969; 3rd edition in 1979, ISBN 3-8009-1279-1)
* **Bibliographic Significance:** This publication constitutes the **editio princeps** (first printing) and facsimile edition of Leibniz's manuscript *De progressione dyadica* (dated March 15, 1679; LH XXXV, 3, 2, 4 folios). 
* **Historical Context:** Leibniz did not publish this 1679 manuscript during his lifetime. C. I. Gerhardt omitted *De progressione dyadica* from his standard 7-volume edition *Die mathematischen Schriften* (1849–1863), where Leibniz's binary mathematics was represented primarily by the later 1703 paper *Explication de l'Arithmétique Binaire*. Thus, the 1966 Siemens volume was the first public printing of the 1679 manuscript text and facsimile.

## 2. Legal Analysis (§ 70 & § 71 UrhG with Dates)

### A. The Underlying 1679 Manuscript
* **Author:** Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716).
* **Copyright Term:** Under German Copyright Law (§ 64 UrhG), copyright expires 70 years after the author's death (*post mortem auctoris*). Leibniz died in 1716; his original writings entered the public domain in 1786.
* **Public Domain Status:** The 1679 Latin text, mathematical formulas, and concepts authored by Leibniz are in the **Public Domain worldwide**.

### B. Protection of Scientific Editions (§ 70 UrhG)
* **Statutory Provision:** German Copyright Act (*Urheberrechtsgesetz* - UrhG) § 70 (*Wissenschaftliche Ausgaben*):
  1. Protects editions of public domain works or texts if they represent the result of scientific editorial/scholarly collation (*wissenschaftlich sichtende Tätigkeit*) and differ significantly from previously known editions.
  2. The right belongs to the editor/author of the edition (§ 70 Abs. 2 UrhG).
  3. **Protection Duration (§ 70 Abs. 3 UrhG):** The right expires **25 years** after publication (or 25 years after production if unpublished within that period).
* **Term Calculation (§ 69 UrhG):** Fristen begin with the conclusion of the calendar year in which the publishing event occurred.
* **Application & Date Computation:**
  * First Publication Year: **1966**
  * Start of Term (§ 69 UrhG): December 31, 1966
  * Expiration Date: **December 31, 1991** (25 calendar years after publication)
  * Even if computed from the 3rd edition (1979), protection under § 70 lapsed on **December 31, 2004**.
* **Scope Restriction:** § 70 protection covers only the editor's specific arrangement, critical apparatus, and scholarly commentary—it never gives copyright over the underlying historic text itself. Regardless, because more than 25 years have elapsed since publication, **all § 70 protection for the 1966 edition has expired**.

### C. Protection of Unpublished Posthumous Works (§ 71 UrhG)
* **Statutory Provision:** UrhG § 71 (*Nachgelassene Werke* / Editio Princeps):
  1. Grants an exclusive right to exploit a previously unpublished work published for the first time after the expiration of copyright.
  2. **Protection Duration (§ 71 Abs. 3 UrhG):** Exactly **25 years** from first lawful publication or public presentation.
* **Application & Date Computation:**
  * First Publication Year: **1966** (Siemens edition)
  * Start of Term (§ 69 UrhG): December 31, 1966
  * Expiration Date: **December 31, 1991**
* **Conclusion on § 71:** Any editio princeps rights created by the 1966 first publication fully expired on December 31, 1991.

### D. Reproductions and Facsimiles (EU Directive 2019/790 & § 68 UrhG)
* Under modern EU and German law (EU Copyright Directive 2019/790, Art. 14, as implemented in German UrhG § 68), faithful photographic or digital reproductions of out-of-copyright visual/textual cultural heritage materials do not trigger new related rights (*Lichtbildschutz*).

### E. Legal Summary
**Transcription is 100% legally defensible.** The 1679 manuscript text is public domain, and all potential statutory protection periods under German law (§ 70 and § 71 UrhG) for the 1966 edition expired over 34 years ago (on December 31, 1991). No third party holds enforceable copyright or related rights in the text or facsimile arrangement of *De progressione dyadica*.
