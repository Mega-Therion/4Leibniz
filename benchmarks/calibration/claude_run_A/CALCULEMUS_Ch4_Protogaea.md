## 1. The Book Leibniz Never Published

Between 1691 and 1693, while serving as court historian to the House of Brunswick-Lüneburg, Leibniz wrote **Protogaea** — a treatise on the origin and geological history of the Earth. He had been commissioned to write a dynastic history; characteristically, he decided to start with the formation of the planet.

Protogaea advanced three revolutionary claims:

1. **The Earth began as a molten globe**, cooling from an incandescent state — anticipating the nebular hypothesis by a century (Laplace, 1796).
2. **Sedimentary rocks record a sequential history** of deposition, erosion, and re-deposition — anticipating stratigraphy and uniformitarianism (Hutton, 1788; Lyell, 1830).
3. **Fossils are the remains of once-living organisms**, not *lusus naturae* (sports of nature) or divine decorations — anticipating paleontology as a science.

Leibniz never published it. The manuscript sat in the Hanover archive until 1749, when it was finally printed — thirty-three years after his death.
